# The night at Liuxiandong is brightly lit with lights.

I used to hear that working overtime in Shenzhen is intense, but I only had a vague impression. It wasn't until after the gathering, when everyone strolled near Liuxiandong, and I looked up and saw the office buildings still brightly illuminated, that I suddenly felt this statement became concrete.

It turns out the night is already so deep, and there are still so many people inside the buildings who haven’t left work.

![Office buildings in Liuxiandong ](/images/pictures/7bed5913069e254862d560e111a71bbaf36360836bc8c769df51553fdc1bc85b.webp)

## The visible "workplace vibe"

When the boss used to motivate employees to work overtime, I wondered: Is overtime really as common as the rumors say in Shenzhen?

That night was a firsthand experience of this.

From downstairs, the windows are cut into distinct sections by the lighting. From a distance, it looks beautiful; the lines of the buildings and the night sky combine to make it even suitable for photography. But when you think about the people sitting behind each light who haven’t finished their work yet, the scene gains a strong "workplace vibe."

![The building with the lights still on ](/images/pictures/dd13d7eec988d7047e6cc5c68fa0966c7cc06af3c21363ef55cfa6f7e8bea282.webp)

![The building that transmits messages at night ](/images/pictures/a981a7c73a7fd636ba1205aba496f81fe62dd6a9b8dcb0f6fc0759bafe6b0107.webp)

During the day, those walking here are just a part of the crowd. At night, the number of people on the streets decreases, but the building remains illuminated. The work rhythm of a city doesn't necessarily require statistical data; sometimes, just looking up at 9 PM gives a good idea.

## The hot and humid air

It’s still very hot at night in Shenzhen.

High buildings, roads, and dense lighting overlap, making the air feel stagnant. Maybe it’s the heat island effect, or perhaps it’s just the usual oppressive heat those days, but when walking downstairs, you can feel a lingering sense of heat.

![Buildings surrounded by lighting ](/images/pictures/e4d8b37e271e42b31454488d87a7cce3e590f18c7b60619c156c3d7eb0544c38.webp)

This oppressive heat, combined with the unextinguished offices around, makes it difficult to completely forget about work. Even if you’ve left your workspace, the environment still reminds you that this area is still in operation.

However, that day's mood was actually one of the more relaxed times I've felt recently.

The dinner party is over, and there are no immediate issues with work, so there's no need for everyone to immediately get back to their computers. We just stroll downstairs to take a look at these buildings that we usually don’t pay much attention to. Work pressure hasn’t disappeared out of thin air, but at least on that short walk, we can shift our focus away from tasks and progress.

![Night view of Youxian Cave](/images/pictures/0e1629d39cbfdc893682c7f3e4157fc34bb3d69765c474bca02890ab7af83642.webp)

## The project is still progressing steadily.

It’s also realistic that we can relax because the Agent project I developed myself hasn’t encountered any issues and is progressing steadily according to plan.

When working on a project, what’s most concerning isn’t often the amount of work, but rather the uncertainty of unexpected events that might arise next. If one part goes awry, it could disrupt everything that follows. Although I’m still busy, at least the project hasn’t deviated from its expectations, and things are moving forward step by step.

This “no problems” situation doesn’t sound like a significant achievement worth celebrating, but when you’re actually involved, you realize how rare stability is. It means the previous judgments held up well, and it also means there’s no need to worry about fixing things later tonight.

So even though the air is hot and the surrounding buildings seem brightly lit as if no one has left work, my mood isn’t tight with it.

## Before returning

As we approached the end of our walk, we passed through a passage surrounded by light strips. Compared to the high buildings outside, it felt quieter, with repetitive arcs leading forward, as if temporarily isolating the noise outside.

![The night passage ](/images/pictures/3ba72ab6150eb49e0ec8e779975587ab564c523ef54a166045effad21241c74d.webp)

The work pace in Shenzhen probably won’t change due to a single dinner or a walk. The next day, when you wake up, the projects will continue, and the office lights may stay on until late.

But that night, for the first time, I saw the "mood" of Liuxiandong very clearly, and amidst this thick, indelible work atmosphere, I found a rare sense of relaxation.

The project moves forward steadily, and I can also slow down for a while.

This is probably the most comforting thing during those hot, oppressive nights.
