# The Night in Liuxiandong: Brightly Lit with Lights

I used to hear that working overtime in Shenzhen is intense, but I only had a vague impression. It wasn’t until after a gathering, when everyone strolled near Liuxiandong, and I looked up and saw the brightly lit office buildings around us, that I suddenly realized this statement became concrete.

It turns out it’s already so late at night, and there are still so many people in the buildings who haven’t left work.

![Office buildings in Liuxiandong](/images/pictures/7bed5913069e254862d560e111a71bbaf36360836bc8c769df51553fdc1bc85b.webp)

## The Obvious "Work Ethic"

When the boss used to encourage employees to work overtime, I used to wonder: Is overtime really as common as the rumors say in Shenzhen?

That night was a firsthand experience of this.

From downstairs, the windows are divided into distinct sections by the lighting. It looks beautiful in the distance, and the lines of the buildings combined with the night sky even makes it suitable for photography. But just thinking about the people sitting behind each light who haven’t finished their work adds a strong "work ethic" to the scene.

![Building with still glowing lights](/images/pictures/dd13d7eec988d7047e6cc5c68fa0966c7cc06af3c21363ef55cfa6f7e8bea282.webp)

![Building that transmits messages at night](/images/pictures/a981a7c73a7fd636ba1205aba496f81fe62dd6a9b8dcb0f6fc0759bafe6b0107.webp)

During the day, people walk here as part of the crowd. At night, the number of people decreases, but the building does not become dark. The work rhythm of a city can be felt without needing statistics; sometimes just looking up at 9 PM gives you a general idea.

## Hot and humid air

It is still very hot at night in Shenzhen.

High buildings, roads, and dense lighting overlap, making the air feel stagnant. Perhaps it’s the heat island effect, or maybe it’s just the usual warmth during those days. In any case, when walking downstairs, you can feel a lingering sense of discomfort.

![Building surrounded by lighting](/images/pictures/e4d8b37e271e42b31454488d87a7cce3e590f18c7b60619c156c3d7eb0544c38.webp)

This oppressive heat, combined with the unextinguished offices around, makes it difficult to completely forget about work. Even if you have left your workspace, the environment still reminds you that this area is still in operation.

However, that day's mood was actually one of the more relaxed times recently.

The dinner party is over, and there are no immediate issues with work, so there’s no need for everyone to immediately get back to their computers. We’re just walking downstairs to take a look at these buildings that we usually don’t pay much attention to. The work pressure hasn’t disappeared out of thin air, but at least on that short walk, we can shift our focus away from tasks and progress.

![Night view of Youxian Cave](/images/pictures/0e1629d39cbfdc893682c7f3e4157fc34bb3d69765c474bca02890ab7af83642.webp)

## The project is still progressing steadily

You can relax because there is a practical reason: the Agent project I developed myself has not encountered problems and is progressing steadily according to plan.

When working on a project, what causes the most anxiety is often not the amount of work, but the fear that something unexpected might happen suddenly. If one part goes awry, it could disrupt everything that follows. Although I am still busy, at least the project has not deviated from its expected path, and things are moving forward step by step.

This "no issues" situation may not seem like a significant achievement worth celebrating, but when you are actually involved, you realize how rare stability is. It means the previous judgments hold up to test, and it also means there's no need to worry about fixing things as we walk tonight.

So even though the air is hot and the surrounding buildings seem brightly lit as if no one has left work, my mood isn't tight with it.

## Before returning

As we were nearing the end of our walk, we passed through a passage surrounded by light strips. Compared to the high buildings outside, it felt quieter, with repetitive arcs leading forward, as if temporarily isolating the noise outside.

![Night Passage](/images/pictures/3ba72ab6150eb49e0ec8e779975587ab564c523ef54a166045effad21241c74d.webp)

The work pace in Shenzhen won’t change just because of a dinner or a walk. The next day, we’ll wake up and the projects will continue, and the office lights may stay on until late at night.

But that night, for the first time, I saw the “mood” of Liuxiandong very clearly, and in this thick, indelible work atmosphere, I also found a rare sense of relaxation.

The project moves forward steadily, and I can also slow down for a while.

This is probably the most comforting thing during that hot, humid night.
